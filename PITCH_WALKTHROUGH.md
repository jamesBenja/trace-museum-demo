# TRACE v1.0 | Museum partnership pitch walkthrough

**Live demo:** https://jamesbenja.github.io/trace-museum-demo/?v=10

## The 90-second story

**0–15 seconds: Meet your guide.** Open the app, choose MMFA or PAMM, and meet **Mica**, an original little spark of paint. Explain that visitors aren't simply reading labels; they're restoring lost artistic powers by discovering works around a museum.

**15–30 seconds: Choose an adventure.** Choose Family Team, enter the fantasy adventure map and point out the first two available mysteries. Visitors choose their order, and the third path unlocks after the first discovery. Make clear that this is a *story map*, not a claim about actual gallery locations.

**30–45 seconds: Find and scan.** Open an artwork clue and show the two cooperative roles: Spotter and Storyteller. Explain that the family is looking at real art together. Use the labelled Demo testing tools or a photo of the artwork on a second screen to exercise the matcher. These are proof-of-concept tests, not evidence of onsite reliability.

**45–70 seconds: Create and learn.** The artwork triggers a reveal, a short art-history story, and a unique interactive activity. Demonstrate MMFA's perspective-line challenge or PAMM's symbol-decoding puzzle, then collect the artistic power.

**70–80 seconds: Unlock the world.** Return to the fantasy map and show that a new path has opened. After two discoveries, a comparative observation bonus appears. The visitor passport retains discoveries, optional photos and an image-free certificate.

**80–90 seconds: Show the museum proposition.** Open the MAP tab for official visitor map links and the separate MUSEUM panel for illustrative curator controls and proposed educational measures. Explain that verified gallery positions, editorial review, privacy consent, full content-management tools, and impact measurement would be developed with the institution.

## Different pitches

**MMFA:** A bilingual-ready self-guided family adventure that helps visitors look carefully at old masters and Canadian art-making techniques. French story drafts exist; full v1 interface localization is pending.

**PAMM:** A next-generation complement to existing family discovery programming, where physical artworks unlock branching interpretation and hands-on creative challenges. Position the three samples around symbols, visual communication and photography.

## Pilot proposal

One gallery or contiguous route, six to eight verified on-view artworks, 20–30 minutes of play, educator-approved scripts, optional audio/text, accessibility alternatives, and a small supervised family test. Learn from completion, learning recall, family conversation and enjoyment. Establish rights clearance and gallery permissions before public operation.

## Critical status

This is an independently created public proof of concept, not a museum-endorsed or on-gallery product. The published collection artworks have not been verified as currently on view. The curator controls are a local mockup, not a connected CMS. Live phone recognition requires field testing. Creative games are educational interpretations and do not reconstruct actual artworks' processes. The public source code is visible on GitHub and should be separated from proprietary production development.
