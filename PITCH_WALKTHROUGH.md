# TRACE v0.7: 90-second museum pitch walkthrough

**Live playable prototype:** https://jamesbenja.github.io/trace-museum-demo/?v=7

**Positioning:** A self-guided discovery layer that transforms curated museum artworks into playable stories, helping families observe, interpret, learn and return.

## Presenter walkthrough (approximately 90 seconds)

**0:00 to 0:15 | Hook**
“Imagine your family visiting a museum. Instead of asking children simply to look at paintings, we give them a mystery that only the artworks themselves can solve. TRACE is a playful companion, not a replacement for looking.”

On-screen: Open TRACE. Choose Montréal (The Lost Colours) or Miami (The Hidden Signals).

**0:15 to 0:35 | The hunt**
“Each chapter begins with a clue to a specific artwork. The visitor sees an enlarged fragment, searches for that work, then uses a phone camera to photograph the full painting.”

On-screen: Begin adventure, open the first clue. If in an office, use the explicitly labeled Studio Demo test option.

**0:35 to 0:55 | The aha moment**
“The scan recognizes the artwork, triggering a reveal and a short artist story. Instead of passive text, the visitor solves a small visual learning challenge, earning a piece of the mystery.”

On-screen: Show verified-artwork result, animated artwork reveal, story and puzzle. Avoid claiming sample-image testing proves live onsite reliability.

**0:55 to 1:05 | The payoff**
“Every discovery enters a personal passport. Three discoveries restore the lost lens and create a take-home keepsake. It becomes something children are proud of, not just a museum checklist.”

On-screen: Open My Finds, show the collectible stamps and downloadable certificate. No artwork images or visitor photos are exported in the certificate.

**1:05 to 1:30 | The institution**
“For museum teams, the same software could support many routes, ages, languages, changing displays and curriculum-led stories. The goal is to give educators tools to author the experience and to evaluate outcomes through privacy-preserving participation data.”

On-screen: Tap Museum. Show the illustrative artwork-selection panel and proposed outcome measures. Explain clearly that this is a mockup, not a connected production CMS or real analytics.

## Pilot proposal

- One pavilion or gallery area, with 6 to 8 artworks that the museum confirms are physically on display and eligible for imagery use.
- Families with children around 6 to 12, with adult-led mobile participation, text/audio accessibility and language options.
- A 20 to 30-minute self-guided experience with a guided usability test and a limited public evaluation.
- Museum educators approve artist interpretation, route, language and accessibility; TRACE provides interaction design, artwork matching and analytics architecture.
- Evaluate engagement, accessibility, completion, short learning recall, enjoyment and repeat-visit intention without unnecessary collection of children's personal information.

**MMFA angle:** An always-available bilingual family activity that encourages looking at permanent-collection works and art-making techniques, complementing existing educational programming.

**PAMM angle:** A camera-enabled, story-responsive extension of museum discovery, complementary to existing offerings such as PAMMPort. Emphasize visual language, creative thinking and local/Caribbean/Latin American artistic connections as appropriate to the museum-approved selection.

## Commercial discussion

Propose a paid or sponsored pilot with agreed implementation deliverables, curatorial approvals and success metrics. A subsequent multi-year licensing discussion should depend on the pilot results. All licensing, implementation fees, support levels and rights must be negotiated.

## Be precise about what exists today

The v0.7 browser prototype includes six sample collection records, working test-image matching, an experimental camera workflow, localized story previews, animated artwork reveals, visual educational puzzles, local progress/passports and an illustrative museum dashboard.

It is **not** a complete production CMS, proven on-gallery recognition service, complete accessibility audit, or museum-endorsed experience. Not all artworks are known to be currently displayed. The live public demo includes source links to the collection artworks; institutional permission is required for a commercial deployment and rights-sensitive promotional reuse.

**Next product-development priorities:** Field photography tests in both institutions, gallery validation, full translation review, curator editing backend, privacy/consent design, route accessibility and a measured pilot.
